## § Semiconductor Industry

The semiconductor industry continues its relentless march forward, pushing the boundaries of physics and materials science. This quarter, much of the discourse centers on TSMC's N2 (2nm) process node, their inaugural foray into Gate-All-Around (GAA) nanosheet transistors. Unlike the incumbent FinFETs, GAA structures fully encapsulate the channel with the gate, providing superior electrostatic control, drastically reducing leakage currents, and enabling greater transistor density. TSMC claims up to a 15% improvement at ISO power compared to previous nodes. However, transitioning a foundational transistor architecture is fraught with challenges. Industry analysts like Triolo highlight SRAM and high-current blocks as critical areas where "early GAA pain" typically manifests, impacting yield and ramp rates.

TSMC is strategically positioning N2 for high-volume production in 2026, targeting both mobile and High-Performance Computing (HPC) segments. Their aggressive expansion, with new facilities in Baoshan and Kaohsiung, aims for a monthly capacity reaching "OOOOO" wafers by late 2025, scaling further to "OOOOO" wafers/month thereafter. This commitment underscores the anticipated demand. Meanwhile, Samsung is emerging as a formidable alternative, with its own 2nm GAA process (SF2P node). They aim to boost 2nm orders by approximately 130%, leveraging their flexible production system to capture customers struggling with TSMC's fully booked advanced node capacity, especially amidst the AI surge. The competitive landscape for advanced logic is intensifying, with both foundries grappling with the multi-billion-dollar R&D and CAPEX requirements, where yield metrics directly translate to billions in profitability. NVIDIA's B200, fabricated on TSMC's 4NP node with an astounding 208 billion transistors, is a testament to the insatiable demand driving these investments.

## § AI Hardware

The dichotomy in AI hardware innovation continues to sharpen, primarily driven by the economics of inference at scale versus on-device efficiency. For datacenter-grade AI, NVIDIA's Blackwell B200 GPU is a behemoth, pushing boundaries with 1440 petaFLOPS (FP4) and 720 petaFLOPS (FP8/FP6) Tensor Core compute. This immense computational power is only as effective as its data delivery system, reflected in its 576TB/s total memory bandwidth (GB200 NVL72 configuration) and 130TB/s total NVLink bandwidth.
